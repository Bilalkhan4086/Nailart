# Product Marketing Context

**Document version:** v1
**Last updated:** 2026-09-27

## Product Overview
**One-liner:** NailMuse by BookMyLook is a UK-focused discovery guide for current nail art designs.
**What it does:** It monitors reputable UK beauty and professional nail publications, turns recurring trends into original summaries, and gives readers salon-ready briefs. Each trend retains a visible source and publication date.
**Product category:** Nail art inspiration, nail trend discovery and beauty editorial.
**Product type:** Free consumer content website.
**Business model:** Audience and organic-search growth for the BookMyLook brand; direct monetisation is not yet defined.

## Target Audience
**Primary audience:** UK consumers looking for nail art inspiration before a salon appointment or at-home manicure.
**Primary use case:** Find a current, wearable nail design and understand what to ask for at a UK salon.
**Jobs to be done:**
- Discover new and trending nail art designs without searching across multiple publications.
- Find ideas suited to a specific nail length, colour, finish, season or skill level.
- Save or describe a design clearly enough to recreate it at a salon or at home.
**Use cases:**
- Autumn and seasonal manicure research.
- Beginner-friendly nail art inspiration.
- Short nails, toenails, rainbow colour and event-specific ideas.

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** Nail inspiration is scattered across social feeds and editorial sites, often without practical instructions or dates.
**Why alternatives fall short:**
- Social posts can lack context, attribution and clear trend timing.
- Editorial round-ups can be lengthy and hard to compare.
- Search results often repeat thin, generic or outdated galleries.
**What it costs users:** Time, uncertainty and salon appointments where the requested finish is hard to explain.
**Emotional tension:** Users want a manicure that feels current and personal without choosing a look they cannot wear or communicate.

## Competitive Landscape
**Direct:** Beauty editorial trend galleries — strong authority but scattered across publications and not designed for comparison.
**Secondary:** Pinterest, Instagram and TikTok — visually rich but inconsistent in attribution, freshness and practical guidance.
**Indirect:** Generic image-search galleries — fast to browse but often thin, duplicated or outdated.

## Differentiation
**Key differentiators:**
- UK-first trend selection and British English.
- Source publication and date shown on every design.
- Original summaries rather than copied articles.
- Practical salon briefs, wear notes and related-design navigation.
**How we do it differently:** NailMuse combines multi-source editorial monitoring with a structured, searchable design library.
**Why that is better:** Visitors can compare trends quickly, understand why they matter and communicate a chosen look clearly.

## Objections
| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| “Is this copied from magazines?” | Every description is original and links to the reporting that informed it. |
| “Will this design suit short nails?” | Shape and wear guidance appear on each trend page. |
| “Are these trends current in the UK?” | The edit is reviewed regularly and displays source dates. |

**Anti-persona:** Readers seeking unverified medical nail advice or exact product guarantees.

### Keyword rules for every future content task
1. Choose one primary keyword per page and match the page’s search intent.
2. Use the primary phrase naturally in the title, H1, first 100 words and meta description when it reads well.
3. Use two to four relevant secondary phrases in body copy, headings, image alt text or internal-link anchors.
4. Do not repeat awkward query variants verbatim in reader-facing sentences. Translate “new nail arts designs” and “nail art design new” into grammatical British English while preserving the topic.
5. Do not create near-duplicate pages for reversed-word-order variants; consolidate them on one useful page.
6. Add genuine value through technique, difficulty, nail shape, colour palette, wear notes, salon wording and current source attribution.
7. Use UK spelling and vocabulary: colour, favourite, autumn, nail technician and salon appointment.
8. Avoid keyword stuffing, hidden text, copied publisher passages and unsupported ranking claims.

**Words and phrases to use:** nail art designs, nail art design, new nail art designs, beginners’ nail art designs, rainbow nail art designs, easy nail art designs for toenails, newest nail art designs, UK nail trends, salon-ready brief.
**Words to avoid:** viral without evidence, guaranteed to rank, best ever, copied source headlines, US-only “fall” when “autumn” is intended.

## Brand Voice
**Tone:** Helpful, current, editorial and trustworthy.
**Style:** Clear British English, concise explanations and practical salon language.
**Personality:** Stylish, informed, accessible and transparent.

## Proof Points
**Current proof:** Multi-source UK editorial monitoring, dated citations, original trend summaries and dedicated trend pages.
**Do not invent:** Traffic, ranking, customer, booking or conversion statistics.

## Goals
**Business goal:** Grow qualified UK organic traffic and connect nail inspiration with the BookMyLook brand.
**Conversion action:** Explore a trend guide and save a design for the next manicure.
**SEO goal:** Build topical authority around nail art designs while earning indexation and engagement through useful, distinct pages.
